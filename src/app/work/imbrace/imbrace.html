Imbrace

Low back pain should not cost you your sense of self. Imbrace was designed to make sure it doesn’t.

Low back pain is one of the most prevalent yet overlooked conditions affecting millennial women today. Unlike acute injuries with clear causes, non-specific low back pain is chronic, recurring, and deeply woven into daily life. Existing solutions sit almost entirely in the medical or preventive space with clinical braces, physiotherapy aids, and posture correctors that were designed to treat a condition, not consider the person wearing them. For a generation that is deeply connected to personal expression and style, being handed a beige medical support is not just uninspiring, it is a barrier to actually using it.

Imbrace was born from this gap. It sits at the crossroads of fashion and medicine — a wearable that offers immediate ease while blending seamlessly into everyday style. It doesn't ask women to compromise. Instead, it invites them to embrace their pain as part of their story, and do something about it on their own terms.

The Opportunity

Millennial women are a generation that moves between offices, social settings, and the demands of an active life outside the home. They are also a generation that shops with intent, values personalisation, and expects the products they use to reflect who they are. The intersection of wearable health and fashion is largely untapped, and Imbrace was conceived to occupy that space, not as a medical product that tolerates style, but as a fashion-forward wearable that also happens to provide real, meaningful relief.

The Design

Imbrace takes its cues from the tradition of tailored clothing; the idea that what you wear should fit you specifically, not approximately your size. Custom fit is central to both the function and the philosophy of the product. A wearable that fits well moves well, and one that moves well gets worn.

To bring this to life digitally, an online configurator was designed as the centrepiece of the e-commerce experience. Rather than selecting from a fixed catalogue, users are guided through a personalisation journey — choosing fit, style, and finish to create a version of Imbrace that feels entirely their own. The configurator bridges the gap between the intimacy of bespoke tailoring and the accessibility of an online purchase, making customisation feel intuitive rather than complex.

The broader e-commerce experience was designed to reinforce the brand’s position at the intersection of fashion and function — visually confident, warm in tone, and built to communicate that Imbrace is something you would want to wear, not just something you have been told to.

The Process

The project was grounded in research from the outset, exploring the lived experience of millennial women with recurring low back pain, understanding what existing solutions got wrong, and identifying where fashion and wearable technology could meaningfully converge. User interviews, market analysis, and a deep dive into the tailoring and fashion industries shaped both the product concept and the design direction.

From research came concept, and from concept came craft — iterating across the wearable design, the configurator experience, and the commercial model that would take Imbrace from project to product.

The Vision

Imbrace is not just a design project. It is a business proposition. The work includes a commercial roadmap built around the opportunity to bring this to market, identifying the pathways to production, distribution, and growth. The vision is a brand that sits confidently at the crossroads of fashion and healthcare — one that proves relief and self-expression are not opposing forces, but complementary ones.
